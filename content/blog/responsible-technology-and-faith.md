Technology can help people find, hear, study, and return to Scripture. It can also create confusion when speed and confidence are mistaken for wisdom or spiritual authority. A responsible faith product needs visible boundaries, not only good intentions.

## 1. Scripture remains identifiable

Passages should show their references and translation. Product-generated commentary, prayer, confession, or declaration should look different from the biblical text that informed it. A user should always be able to tell what is Scripture and what is not.

## 2. Generated language is never called God's voice

Artificial intelligence may help understand a topic, organize material, or draft a response. It cannot be presented as divine speech. Phrases such as “God is telling you” create spiritual authority that the system does not possess.

Clear labels are part of theological seriousness. They give people the information needed to read with discernment.

## 3. Review is a product feature

When technology assists with sensitive content, review cannot be an invisible aspiration. Teams need documented sources, editorial checks, correction routes, and clear ownership. Higher-risk topics - including crisis, abuse, health, and self-harm - require dedicated safety language and escalation paths.

If a review process does not yet exist, the content should not be described as reviewed.

## 4. Users keep agency

A faith product should help a person choose how to engage instead of trapping them in an endless feed. Reading, listening, prayer, repetition, confession, journaling, and silence can be offered as options rather than optimized for compulsive use.

Users should be able to report a concern, correct context, leave an experience, and understand what happens to anything they submit.

## 5. Privacy follows the sensitivity of the moment

What a person is facing can reveal health, relationship, financial, or spiritual information. A responsible MVP collects as little as possible. Marketing analytics should never receive typed prayer content or private reflections. Retention should be limited, access controlled, and deletion possible.

## Trust is not an afterthought

The deepest product choices are often quiet: labels, references, consent, review, limits, and correction. They may not appear in a launch headline, but they determine whether a tool deserves confidence.

FaithCine Selah is still in design and development. Our stated direction is to make Scripture traceable, technology humble, and trust practices visible from the beginning. As the product grows, those intentions will need to become documented, tested operations.
